How to work out how many tiles you need

Buying tiles accurately means converting room and tile sizes to the same unit, adding realistic cut-and-breakage waste for your layout pattern, and rounding up to whole cartons.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Measure the length and width of the floor (or wall width and height). Break L-shaped rooms into rectangles and add their areas.
  2. Check the actual nominal and work size of one tile on the carton specification—for instance, a "12 × 12 in" tile may be slightly smaller to allow grout joints.
  3. Choose a waste allowance matching your layout: 10% for straight grid layouts, 12% to 15% for brick or diagonal, and 15% to 20% for herringbone or complex cuts.
  4. Enter how many whole tiles come in one carton so the final order rounds up to full boxes.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room or wall length: Measure the longest span of the tiled rectangle.
  • Room or wall width: Measure perpendicular width (or height for a wall).
  • Tile length: Nominal or actual tile length from the box spec.
  • Tile width: Nominal or actual tile width from the box spec.
  • Layout waste allowance: 10% straight grid; 15% diagonal; 15–20% herringbone.
  • Tiles per box: Number of whole tiles packed inside one carton.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ordering exact net floor area with zero waste. Even simple rectangular rooms lose tiles to perimeter edge cuts and accidental breakage.
  • Using 10% waste on a diagonal or herringbone layout, which creates triangular cuts along every single wall.
  • Using every last tile during installation and keeping no spares—matching the exact shade and caliber years later is often impossible.
  • Mixing cartons with different dye-lot (tonality) or caliber codes on the same floor or wall.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Check the carton label for exact square feet or square meters per box and confirm it matches piece count × tile area.
  • Set aside 5% to 10% of leftover intact tiles in storage for future repairs.
  • Account separately for bullnose trim, mosaic borders, and niches.
