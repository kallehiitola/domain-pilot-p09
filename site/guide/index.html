How to work out how many tiles you need

Calculate rectangular floor area, add a visible waste allowance and round to whole tiles and boxes. See how carton rounding changes the purchase quantity.

Quick answer: A 4 × 3 m room with 30 × 30 cm tiles and 10% waste needs 147 tiles. Ten-tile boxes require 15 boxes, supplying 150 tiles.

Step by step

  1. Measure the length and width of the floor, or the width and height of the wall, to find the area.
  2. Read the actual tile size from the box or product page.
  3. Add a waste allowance for cuts and breakage. Diagonal and herringbone layouts need more than a straight layout.
  4. Divide the total by the number of tiles in a box and round up to whole boxes.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Room length
  • Room width
  • Tile length
  • Tile width
  • Waste allowance
  • Tiles per box

How the calculation works

Divide room length times width by tile length times width, with all areas expressed in the same units. Multiply by one plus your waste percentage and round up to whole tiles. Divide by tiles per carton and round up again to whole cartons.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering the exact area with no allowance for cuts.
  • Using every tile. Keep a few spares for future repairs, because the same shade may be hard to find later.
  • Mixing boxes with different batch or shade codes.

Another worked example

A 3 × 3 m floor with no waste allowance needs 100 tiles, or 10 boxes of 10.

What changes the estimate

This is an area estimate, not a tile cutting layout. Diagonal patterns, grout joints, broken tiles, niches and small edge cuts affect waste. Choose the allowance for your layout and confirm carton coverage and tile dimensions with the supplier.
